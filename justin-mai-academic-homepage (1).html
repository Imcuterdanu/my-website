Short Bio

I am a PhD Research Fellow in the Mitchell Lab at the University of Idaho, studying neuroinflammation and glia–neuron interactions in the retina. My current research examines the immunometabolic profiles that drive microglial responses in vertebrate retinas, and I am a 2024 National Science Foundation (NSF) Graduate Research Fellow. Before coming to UI, I studied retinal connectomics at San Francisco State University (NIH-URISE Fellowship) and cell-therapy techniques at the Vitalant Research Institute (CIRM Research Fellowship). I am passionate about advancing sight-restoration science and making the life sciences more accessible to blind and visually impaired students.

Research Interests

  • Neuroinflammation in the retina: Its role in retinal disease and how microglia detect injury and coordinate immune responses in the eye.
  • Glia–neuron interactions: How microglia and Müller cells influence neuronal health, function, and regeneration.
  • Retinal regeneration: Photoreceptor function and why photoreceptors can be replaced in some species — and what that teaches us for human vision repair.
