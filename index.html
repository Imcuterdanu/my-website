Short Bio

I am a PhD Research Fellow in the Mitchell Lab at the University of Idaho, studying neuroinflammation and glia–neuron interactions in the retina. My current research examines the immunometabolic profiles that drive microglial responses in vertebrate retinas, and I am a 2024 National Science Foundation (NSF) Graduate Research Fellow. Before coming to UI, I studied retinal connectomics at San Francisco State University (NIH-URISE Fellowship) and cell-therapy techniques at the Vitalant Research Institute (CIRM Research Fellowship). I am passionate about advancing sight-restoration science and making the life sciences more accessible to blind and visually impaired students.

Research Interests

  • Neuroinflammation in the retina: Its role in retinal disease and how microglia detect injury and coordinate immune responses in the eye.
  • Glia–neuron interactions: How microglia and Müller cells influence neuronal health, function, and regeneration.
  • Retinal regeneration: Photoreceptor function and why photoreceptors can be replaced in some species — and what that teaches us for human vision repair.

Research Experience

  • NSF Graduate Research Fellow
    University of Idaho, Mitchell Lab · 2024–present

    Project: Immunometabolic Profiles Driving Microglial Responses and Functions in the Vertebrate Retina.

    PhD research on neuroinflammation, microglial activation, and glia–neuron signaling in retinal disease models.

    PI: Dr. Diana Mitchell

    • Microglia-Specific GeneWeld HDR: Developing and validating a GeneWeld-based homology-directed repair (HDR) strategy for microglia-specific genetic engineering in zebrafish to enable targeted manipulation of microglial function.
    • IRG1–Itaconate Signaling in Retinal Microglia: Characterizing the temporal dynamics of IRG1 reporter expression following photoreceptor injury and investigating its potential role in microglial immunometabolic responses during retinal damage and regeneration.
  • NIH-URISE Fellow
    San Francisco State University, Anastassov Lab · 2022–2024

    Project: Structural Diversity of Mitochondria and Quantification of Synaptic Vesicles from Different Neuron Types in the Simplex Retina of Little Skate.

    Undergraduate research in retinal connectomics, including 3D reconstruction of mitochondria and ribbon synapses.

    PI: Dr. Ivan Anastassov

    • Mitochondrial Diversity and Synaptic Vesicles in Little Skate Retina: Characterizing mitochondrial morphology and quantifying synaptic vesicles in retinal neurons using 3D electron microscopy.

    Poster — ARVO 2023

  • CIRM Research Fellow
    Vitalant Research Institute, Stem Cell Therapy Core · 2019–2020

    Project: Reforming the Enumeration of Clonogenic Frequency Method in Mesenchymal Stromal Cell Population: Extreme Limiting Dilution Assay vs Colony Forming Unit Assay.
